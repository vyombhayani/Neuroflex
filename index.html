Natural movement, independent learning

R2 learns each finger independently. There are no straight/bent capture steps here. Percentages come directly from the glove.

Move comfortably through your usual range and return. LEARNING, RETURN, HIGH/CHECK and LOW/CHECK are retained as statuses; unavailable percentages are shown as gaps.

A percentage describes the sensor’s observed range, not a joint angle or force. Different sessions may learn different endpoints.

Patient-controlled recording

Select a patient to start a 30-second session automatically. Every recorded frame carries that patient and session ID. The ESP32 stops capture after 30 seconds and confirms when queued samples have saved.

The glove stops capture after 30 seconds even if this page closes. Reopening this browser tab does not automatically create a duplicate session. Record again starts another session. A board restart interrupts capture and clears unsent RAM samples.

About 60 seconds of unsent samples fit in the initially empty RAM queue. A full queue drops new samples and counts them. Power loss clears that queue. Saved cloud sessions remain.

How this prototype connects

01 / SENSING

Four flex sensors

GPIO1, GPIO2, GPIO4 and GPIO5. Original R2 sampling, filtering and independent learning.

02 / STORAGE

Wi-Fi to Firebase

Authenticated HTTPS uploads save all four readings together. A bounded RAM queue retries short outages, preserving each sample’s patient and session ID.

03 / FEEDBACK

Live and saved graphs

The dashboard reads saved measurements, shows sensor status and exports session CSVs. No servo or screen required.

Project rationale

Support practice outside the clinic

Home rehabilitation may be difficult to measure consistently. A glove-based approach aims to help capture repetitions, movement patterns and trends while allowing therapists to interpret progress.

Beyond exercise counting

Track per-finger motion instead of relying only on overall repetitions.

Designed around constraints

Four sensors and an ESP32-S3 provide movement readings without a servo or display.

Clear human feedback

Readable trends and summaries support discussion with a physiotherapist.

Research and educational prototype. Movement percentages describe the learned sensor range; clinical interpretation requires separate validation.
